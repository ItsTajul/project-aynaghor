AYNAGHAR: DAYS OF SILENCE

An Interactive Simulator by Md Tajul Islam & Kaniz Fatema

Between January 2009 and August 2024, state security forces in Bangladesh systematically abducted thousands of citizens into a network of secret black sites known as 'Aynaghar' (The House of Mirror).

This interactive story reconstructs the documented conditions inside secret custody and the enduring legal struggles families face outside.

Inside the Cell. Your blindfold is loosened inside the locked room, but your hands remain cuffed behind your back to an iron bed. The space around you is pitch black.

In total darkness, your mind clings to any sound past the exhaust fan's continuous hum...

A deep roar echoes periodically overhead from above the ceiling...

You recognize the sound: a commercial airliner descending toward Dhaka Airport.

"During the day I could hear aeroplanes and the railway crossing, and I counted the passing days by scratching marks on the wall." — Survivor Code BHHG (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 103, 213)

Knowing your cell is located near the airport provides a vital environmental anchor... How do you handle daily confinement?

Relatives search police stations to file a General Diary. Officers refuse to register it, citing "orders from above" and demanding RAB and DB names be removed.

INSIDE: CUSTODY (DAY 14)

Your yelling brings guards to your cell. They unlock the door, hit you with batons, and remove your blanket and pillow.

"When I tried to sleep or yelled, someone came and said, 'Why are you sleeping?'... In the middle of winter, they removed the blanket and pillow altogether..." — Survivor Code BHFJ (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 82–83)

OUTSIDE: FAMILY & LOVED ONES

Your family faces total police refusal. Officers tell them it is a "political matter" beyond jurisdiction, leaving relatives to search hospitals and morgues.

"Police refused our GD unless we removed the names of RAB and DB from the complaint form." — Sanjida Islam Tuli (Sister of Saiful Islam Shyamal & Coordinator of Mayer Daak)
MEALS IN SECRET CUSTODY (DAY 21)

A small flap at the bottom of the cell door opens. A plastic tray with cold rice and thin curry is slid across the floor.

Detainees in secret cells are routinely served half the rations of guards, eating with handcuffed hands in darkness. How do you respond?

INSIDE: CUSTODY (DAY 21)

You eat what little food is provided. In total darkness, meals become one of the few indicators of passing days or special calendar events.

"On special days they served special meals, which made us realize that it was Eid." — Survivor Code BHFI (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 49)

OUTSIDE: FAMILY & LOVED ONES

Your family endures endless bureaucratic shuttling—visiting RAB headquarters, DB offices, and courts for years without receiving any official answer.

"We searched every station and court. Officers told us to go home and wait for a phone call that never came." — Minu Akhter (Wife of Kawser Hossain, disappeared 2013)

INSIDE: CUSTODY (DAY 21)

Late one night, a young guarding soldier quietly slides his own full meal through the door flap to you.

"One soldier in his mid-twenties coped by quietly giving his own full meals to captives who received only half-rations... A captive tearfully thanked him, prompting the soldier to break down crying in silence." — Commission Investigation (Vol. 1, p. 139–140)

OUTSIDE: FAMILY & LOVED ONES

On the outside, authorities use family members as leverage, holding relatives in custody to force surrenders or prevent public protests.

STAGE 3: THE INTERROGATION ROOM (DAY 45)

45 days of secret incommunicado custody have passed. You are re-blindfolded with a jom tupi, dragged down a corridor, and seated at a metal table.

Interrogators remove your blindfold under blinding fluorescent lights, forcing you to look at a document.

An officer slides a pre-scripted Section 164 Statement across the table, confessing to fabricated terrorism charges under the Anti-Terrorism Act.

"Sign it now, or you will be taken to an embankment tonight for a Golf Operation (Extrajudicial 'Crossfire' Execution)."

STAGE 3 OUTCOME: GOLF OPERATION (EXTRAJUDICIAL EXECUTION)

You refused to sign the forced confession. Late that night, you were re-blindfolded, driven to an embankment, and extrajudicially executed in a staged "crossfire" gunfight.

"If you say this, you will get out. If you refuse, we will put you in crossfire here and you will die." — Captor Threat (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 118)

STAGE 4: THE MAGISTRATE APPEARANCE (DAY 180)

After nearly six months (180 days) of secret custody, captors prepare to "legalise" your arrest. They trim your beard, provide clean clothes, and warn you against disclosing secret detention to the judge.

Standing before the magistrate, plainclothes officers watch you from the back of the courtroom.

The magistrate asks if you have anything to say. What do you do?
