AYNAGHAR: DAYS OF SILENCE

An interactive simulation of state-sponsored secret detention in Bangladesh (2009–2024), grounded in victim testimonies from the Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report (Vol. 1).

Inside the Cell. Your blindfold is loosened inside the locked room, but your hands remain cuffed behind your back to an iron bed. The space around you is pitch black, with a constant low hum echoing through the air.

In total darkness, your mind clings to any sound past the exhaust fan's continuous hum...

A deep roar echoes periodically overhead from above the ceiling...

You recognize the sound: a commercial airliner descending toward Dhaka Airport.

"During the day I could hear aeroplanes and the railway crossing, and I counted the passing days by scratching marks on the wall." — Survivor Code BHHG (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 103, 213)

Knowing your cell is located near the airport provides a vital environmental anchor... How do you handle daily confinement?

By staying quiet and performing daily prayers inside your cell, you maintain internal focus. Guards leave you alone, allowing you to track time using aircraft schedules.

"At midnight, I performed ablution and stood reciting the Qur'an in my cell... A guarding soldier told me, 'I prayed all night so they wouldn't put you in crossfire.'" — Survivor Code DFE (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 140)

Your yelling brings guards marching down the corridor. They unlock the door, strike you with batons, and remove your blanket and pillow.

"When I tried to sleep or yelled, someone came and said, 'Why are you sleeping?'... In the middle of winter, they removed the blanket and pillow altogether and made me sit without a chair..." — Survivor Code BHFJ (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 82–83)
MEALS IN SECRET CUSTODY (DAY 7)

A small flap at the bottom of the cell door opens. A plastic tray with cold rice and thin curry is slid across the floor.

Detainees in secret cells are routinely served half the rations of guards, eating with handcuffed hands in darkness.

You eat what little food is provided. In total darkness, meals become one of the few indicators of passing days or special calendar events.

"On special days they served special meals, which made us realize that it was Eid." — Survivor Code BHFI (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 49)

Late one night, a young guarding soldier quietly slides his own full meal through the door flap to you.

"One soldier in his mid-twenties coped by quietly giving his own full meals to captives who received only half-rations... A captive tearfully thanked him for finally getting a full meal, prompting the soldier to break down crying in silence." — Commission Investigation (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 139–140)
STAGE 2: THE INTERROGATION ROOM (DAY 45)

45 days of secret incommunicado custody have passed. You are re-blindfolded with a jom tupi, dragged down a corridor, and seated at a metal table.

Interrogators remove your blindfold under blinding fluorescent lights, forcing you to look at a document.

An officer slides a pre-scripted Section 164 Statement across the table, confessing to fabricated terrorism charges under the Anti-Terrorism Act.

"If you say this, you will get out. If you refuse, we will put you in crossfire here and you will die." — Captor Threat (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 118)
STAGE 3: THE MAGISTRATE APPEARANCE (DAY 180)

After nearly six months (180 days) of secret custody, captors prepare to "legalise" your arrest. They trim your beard, provide clean clothes, and warn you against disclosing secret detention to the judge.

"If you tell the magistrate you were arrested three months ago, we will file five more cases against you. Say you were arrested today." — Captor Warning (Enforced Disappearance Commission Final Report Vol. 1, p. 102)

Standing before the magistrate, plainclothes officers watch you from the back of the courtroom.

The magistrate asks if you have anything to say. What do you do?

PRISON REMAND & PRE-TRIAL DETENTION (DAY 250)

Legal Reality (Commission Report Vol. 1, p. 102)
"Court appearances rarely resulted in freedom. Even when victims exposed their disappearance, magistrates routinely granted police remand anyway, transferring victims to central prison under fabricated charges."
